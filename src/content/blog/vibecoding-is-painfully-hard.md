I’m sick of twitter and linkedIn posts telling how cool it is to create an app from a single prompt. Usually, these posts contain a video, showing how dev drops a one sentence into text-to-code like Bolt or Cursor and gets beautifully designed product.

It works pretty well for hype, impressions and works with clickbait headlines to catch a lot of attention. What they actually not show you.

1) This isn’t functional app, but the AI’s understanding of the idea. Buttons can be fake, no backend, no business logic, just beautiful frontend with couple features.

2) How long did it take to refine a good prompt and how many attempts were recorded before there?

These videos impress those who either didn’t try to vibecode, or vibecoded todo-lists and note-takers, but not complex applications.

I’m not technical. I worked as an analyst and product manager. I don’t understand the code. And my months of vibecoding experience tell me that it could be as demoralizing as any other life experience , but putting more pressure doesn’t necessarily bring u results right away.

This meme pictures it perfectly:

For example, I had 10-20 situations where I stuck with the bug, and tried to fix it for hours, days, and even weeks. It’s endless grind, whatever tools u use, models are the same, and sometimes they just can’t fix the issue.

You click “resolve an issue” 10 times and the error still appears. After that u copy whole project, drop the code into GPT or Claude, and start playing around it, chatting, asking questions, searching for the cause. It easily can take days.

The day I write this note, I spend 6 hours trying to fix image rendering and didn’t succeed. I tried every fixing method I know. I even started learning to code, read the code, dive into documentation, and someone will say, that vibecoding is pure fun and easy. That’s not true. You will have ur victories and failures constantly. And it will be painful, as it always was.

Probably in a couple of years, I will laugh about this note, because AI will become much smarter and will be able to resolve any issue, faster than senior programmer. But in the middle of 2025, it just can’t.
